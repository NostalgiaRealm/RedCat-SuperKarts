## 5. Frame-rate-independent behavior

The original appears to have a rendering cap around 31–32 FPS. Measure and verify this, and separately determine how its simulation timing works. Do not assume the rendering cap equals the simulation update rate.

Remove the port's artificial 31–32 FPS rendering limit. Support smooth rendering at the rate available from the browser and display.

Decouple simulation from rendering. Choose simulation timing and update rules based on the original behavior, with interpolation where appropriate. Do not arbitrarily change the simulation to 60 Hz or blindly multiply every original calculation by delta time.

Physics, AI, race clocks, cameras, animations, pickups, effect durations, and audio triggers must remain consistent as rendering frame rate changes.

Use repeatable input sequences to compare behavior at the measured original rate, 60, 120, 144, and 240 FPS, plus fluctuating frame times and temporary stalls. Where hardware cannot display a requested rate, use controlled timing tests and distinguish them from actual display testing.

Explicitly test:

- Reversing from a standstill.
- Sustained reverse movement.
- Steering while reversing.
- Braking from forward movement and entering reverse, according to the original control behavior.
- Returning from reverse to forward movement.

Compare movement, speed, handling, and timing over equal durations. A kart merely appearing to move backward is insufficient.

Handle focus loss, suspension, and resumption without uncontrolled simulation jumps or stuck inputs.

## 6. Platforms and controls

Target desktop browsers on Linux, Windows, and macOS, plus mobile browsers on Android and iOS. Cover the relevant Chrome/Chromium, Firefox, and Safari environments where available.

Support:

- Keyboard controls.
- Controllers through the Gamepad API, including menu navigation, racing, appropriate analog input and deadzones, and connection/disconnection handling.
- Touch controls with simultaneous inputs, so steering can be combined with acceleration, braking/reverse, and item use.

Document the controls. Keep original control behavior where applicable and make platform adaptations explicit.

Implement responsive menus and HUD layouts for desktop, tablet, and phone screens.

Support both landscape and portrait orientation. In portrait mode, rearrange original artwork, menus, HUD elements, and touch controls into a usable vertical layout. Preserve artwork proportions and keep essential information and controls visible without obscuring critical gameplay.

Handle orientation changes and screen safe areas.

## 7. Widescreen, resolution, and settings

Support widescreen and high-resolution rendering without stretching the scene or original interface artwork.

Preserve the original camera framing at its original aspect ratio. Adapt wider views deliberately and document the field-of-view behavior.

Add persistent settings for:

- Resolution selection, including Auto/native and suitable explicit presets.
- Render scale from 25% to 200%.
- Audio and control settings relevant to the port.

For the browser build, the resolution selector controls the base 3D render resolution. Define 100% render scale as that selected base resolution; the percentage scales both its width and height.

Display the effective rendering dimensions. Account for device pixel ratio without applying scaling twice. Keep menu and HUD readability independent of the 3D render scale, and preserve the viewport's correct aspect ratio.
